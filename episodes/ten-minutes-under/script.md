# Ten Minutes Under: script (stage 2, for GATE 1)

Slug: `ten-minutes-under` · 9:16 · English, US audience, metric units · calm instructor tone.

**Premise (WHO + CONSTRAINT + CLOCK + RESOURCE):** Eli, an adult ice angler, takes a shortcut past
a creek mouth on a frozen Minnesota lake at sunset and falls through six-centimeter ice. Clock:
1-10-1 (one minute to breathe, about ten minutes to get out, about an hour before passing out).
Resource: his jacket full of trapped air, the ice picks around his neck, his own footprints.

**Point of view: first person, past tense.** It is the story of one adult's mistake told after the
fact, so the voice never invites the viewer ("you") onto the ice; the rescue steps switch to short
imperatives only, like the channel's "Rule number…" lines.

**Name and clock:** Eli is named on screen from line 2 (callout `ELI`) and out loud in line 15
("Eli!", called by his brother). On-screen clock: `DEC · 16:18` → at the fall it becomes
`IN WATER 00:00` → `MIN 1` → `MIN 4 · OUT` → back to `16:27` for the cliffhanger.

## Narration (paste into ElevenLabs as ONE block)

```
When the ice broke under me, I had ten minutes. Not seconds.

December, sunset, a frozen Minnesota lake. I was walking back from my brother's fishing shack.

I cut past the creek and never checked the ice. Over moving water, it was six centimeters.

The Minnesota DNR says under ten centimeters of new clear ice, stay off. White ice? About half as strong.

One crack. Then black water, near zero degrees, up to my chest.

Most people think they have seconds, so they panic and thrash. Or they think a heavy jacket drags you down, and tear it off.

Both mistakes burn your time. Rescue experts call it one, ten, one. One minute: get your breathing under control.

Then about ten minutes of strength to get out. And about one hour before you pass out. That hour is not a rest.

So for one minute, I only breathed. My jacket stayed on. Trapped air held me up.

Which way? Step one: turn back the way you came. That ice already held you.

Step two: arms on the unbroken ice. I had ice picks around my neck for exactly this.

Step three: kick until you're flat, dig in the picks, and slide forward on your stomach.

Step four: don't stand up. Roll away from the hole, arms and legs spread wide.

Minute four. Soaked, shaking, out. Step five: get somewhere warm and call nine-one-one.

Then I heard my name. "Eli!" My brother saw it all from the shack. He was running straight at the hole.

The first rule of helping someone? Never rush to the hole. Part two.
```
