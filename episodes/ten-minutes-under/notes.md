## Sources used (fetched 2026-11-01)

Primary (one organisation, Minnesota DNR):

1. **Minnesota DNR, "What if you fall in?"**: https://www.dnr.state.mn.us/safety/ice/survival.html
   Self-rescue steps, in order: don't remove winter clothing ("Heavy clothes won't drag you down,
   but instead can trap air to provide warmth and flotation"); "Turn toward the direction you
   came. That's probably the strongest ice."; hands and arms on the unbroken surface (nails,
   sharpened screwdrivers or ice picks give traction); "Kick your feet and dig in your ice picks
   to work your way back onto the solid ice."; "Lie flat on the ice once you are out and roll
   away from the hole"; get to a warm, dry, sheltered area, re-warm, seek medical attention in
   moderate to severe cases.
2. **Minnesota DNR, "Cold Water Kills" brochure (PDF, 2018)**:
   https://files.dnr.state.mn.us/education_safety/safety/boatwater/cold-water.pdf
   "1-10-1 PRINCIPLE": 1 minute, get breathing under control; 10 minutes of meaningful movement
   (self-rescue if possible, signal or call for help); 1 hour (or more) of useful consciousness,
   focus on slowing heat loss. Same brochure: cold shock (gasping, hyperventilation, panic)
   "within the first 2-3 minutes"; swim failure "within the first 30 minutes"; hypothermia
   "after at least 30 minutes"; "Keep boots and clothes on"; call 911 for anyone pulled from
   cold water.
3. **Minnesota DNR, ice thickness guidelines**: https://www.dnr.state.mn.us/safety/ice/thickness.html
   New clear ice only: under 4 in (10 cm) stay off; 4 in (10 cm) on foot; 5-7 in (13-18 cm)
   snowmobile / small ATV; 7-8 in (18-20 cm) side-by-side; 9-10 in (23-25 cm) small car / SUV;
   11-12 in (28-30 cm) medium SUV / small truck; 13 in (33 cm) medium truck; 16-17 in (40-43 cm)
   heavy-duty truck. White ice is "only about half as strong as new, clear ice"; double the
   guidelines on white ice; thickness varies across a lake; the DNR does not measure ice.
   (This matches the researcher's CORRECTED figures; the old "8-12 in car / 12-15 in truck" is
   not used anywhere.)
4. **Minnesota DNR, ice safety main page**: https://www.dnr.state.mn.us/safety/ice/index.html
   "There is no such thing as 100 percent safe ice."; "Ice formed over flowing water and
   currents is often dangerous." (supports the creek-mouth setup and the ladder footer chip)
5. **Minnesota DNR, "Helping someone else"**: https://www.dnr.state.mn.us/safety/ice/saving.html
   Call 911 first; "Resist the urge to run up to the edge of the hole. This would most likely
   result in two victims in the water.";
   Preach, Reach, Throw, Row, Go; non-professionals should not go onto the ice unless all other
   options are ruled out. (Supports line 16 and sets up Part 2.)

Cross-check (rescue organisation):

6. **Canadian Red Cross (Manitoba), "Safety On and Around the Ice" (PDF, 2014)**:
   https://cdn.redcross.ca/prodmedia/crc/documents/Where-We-Work/Canada/Manitoba/safety-tips-on-and-around-ice-2014.pdf
   Use trapped air in clothing to float on your stomach; "Once your breathing has calmed down,
   reach forward on to the broken ice without pushing down. Kick your legs to push your torso on
   to the ice."; crawl on your stomach or roll away with arms and legs spread; "Don't stand up!";
   head for shore, "usually the direction you came from"; if you can't get out, lift yourself as
   far as you can and don't move (clothes freeze to the ice); call 911 for hypothermia.

Attribution: the 1-10-1 principle is credited to Dr. Gordon Giesbrecht (University of Manitoba)
per facts-01.md (McGill OSS article). The narration says "rescue experts" and the on-screen chip
says "CITED BY MN DNR", so no real person is named in the voice or drawn on screen.
The Canadian Red Cross 2019 blog page and coldwaterbootcamp.com returned 403 / 404 to the fetcher.

## Differences between sources (flagged)

1. **Where to climb out.** DNR: turn toward where you came from, because that ice held you.
   Canadian Red Cross: "Resist the immediate urge to climb back out where you fell in. The ice is
   weak in this area," and mentions the direction you came from only for heading to shore. These
   can be read as compatible (face the direction you came from, reach onto the strongest edge on
   that side), but they are not identical. The script uses the DNR wording. Please confirm you
   are happy with that before the voice is recorded.
2. **Timings.** The DNR brochure prints 1-10-1 (1 min / 10 min / 1 hour or more) AND, on the
   same page, cold shock "within the first 2-3 minutes" and swim failure "within the first 30
   minutes". The script always says "about" for ten minutes and one hour and never promises a
   fixed number. The hook ("ten minutes. Not seconds.") rounds the 10-minute window; the first
   minute is itself the most dangerous part, which line 7 covers ("One minute: get your
   breathing under control").
3. **The hour is conditional.** The DNR brochure says "IF WEARING A LIFE JACKET, THE 1-10-1
   PRINCIPLE MAY SAVE YOUR LIFE!" and "Without a life jacket, most cold water victims die long
   before they become hypothermic." Eli wears no life jacket, so the script adds "That hour is
   not a rest" and never shows waiting as an option. Consider a life jacket / float suit tip in
   Part 2.
4. **If you can't get out.** Canadian Red Cross adds "lift yourself out as far as you can and
   don't move" (clothes freeze to the ice). DNR survival page doesn't say this. Not in the script
   (Eli gets out); a candidate for Part 2.
5. **Improvised picks.** DNR also lists "nails, sharpened screwdrivers". We show only purpose-made
   ice picks with guards on a cord (kids watch; no sharpened tools).
6. **Rewarming.** DNR adds the cold "after-drop" warning and medical care. We stay at "get
   somewhere warm and call 911" per the brief; no medical steps.

## Doubtful points (please check)

- "Most people think they have seconds… think a heavy jacket drags you down": framing, not a
  statistic. The jacket myth is implied by the DNR wording ("Heavy clothes won't drag you
  down"); panic is DNR's own cold-shock description. No number is claimed.
- "Near zero degrees": water under lake ice is near 0 °C (physically about 0-4 °C). Not taken
  from the fetched official pages; low risk, shown as `≈0 °C`.
- "Six centimeters", "minute four", the 3 m roll distance, the 60 m brother distance, and the
  16:18-16:27 clock are story values, not facts. Sunset in northern Minnesota in early December
  is around 16:20-16:30 (not sourced; close enough for a story clock).
- "Never rush to the hole" paraphrases the DNR page's exact sentence: "Resist the urge to run up
  to the edge of the hole. This would most likely result in two victims in the water." (checked
  on a second fetch). Paraphrase is fair; the on-screen stamp could quote "DON'T RUN TO THE HOLE"
  if you want it closer to the source.
- The episode is ~107 s at 2.6 words/s (99 s at 2.7). Survival-short's target is 85-95 s; the
  brief's 230-280 words at 2.4-2.7 w/s gives 85-117 s, so we are inside the brief but on the long
  side. Trim candidates are listed in script.md.

## Safety flags

- **Walking on ice.** Eli is an adult with gear; his walk is framed as a mistake (shortcut, never
  checked, ice over moving water). The only thickness line said aloud is negative ("under ten
  centimeters… stay off"). Vehicle rungs on the ladder stay small and grey; the footer says
  `NO ICE IS 100% SAFE`. No line says or shows "ten centimeters is safe".
- **In the water.** Only Eli is ever in the water; his head and shoulders always stay above the
  water line; no underwater POV, no going under, no distress close-ups (the panic is shown on a
  dashed "ghost" double, then crossed out).
- **Rescue steps.** Only steps stated by the MN DNR and/or Canadian Red Cross; each step's
  wording is in the table above.
- **Rewarming.** "Get somewhere warm and call nine-one-one" only. No medical procedures.
- **Cliffhanger.** The brother is frozen mid-stride before anything happens; no second break is
  shown. Part 2 teaches the DNR "Preach, Reach, Throw" safe-helping sequence and "call 911 first".
- **Real people / brands.** Dr. Giesbrecht is not named aloud or drawn; "MINNESOTA DNR" appears
  as plain text attribution (no logo). Ice picks, phone and clothing are unbranded.
- **Tone.** Calm, no shock stingers, silence during the freeze instead of a scare sound.

## Originality vs "14 Minutes"

- Opening shot: top-down boot print with a crack on a pink frozen lake (14 Minutes opened on a
  phone-alert ECU, then Times Square). Location: open frozen lake vs NYC street and basement.
- Solo hero vs a crowd; cold and daylight-to-blue-hour vs red emergency light and candles.
- New device: the world "freezes" (monochrome, `PAUSED` timer) while the 1-10-1 rule is
  explained, then thaws back into real time.
- New HUD (ice / water / 1-10-1 phase) instead of IMPACT / FOOD / RADIATION.
- Ending: a person (the brother running at the hole) instead of the chocolate joke; the last
  frame rhymes with the first (a boot print from above).
- All narration written new; no music bed; 4 SFX built from scratch.
