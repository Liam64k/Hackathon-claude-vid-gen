# Don't Run to Him: script (stage 2)

Slug: `how-to-help-a-friend-2611071750` · 9:16 · English, US audience, metric units · calm instructor tone.
Art pack: Winter Lake (SIDE / CUT / TOP / boot-print views; Eli + his brother only).

**Premise:** When Eli's brother broke through the ice by the creek mouth at sunset, Eli was forty
meters away with only his phone and the ice picks around his neck. His brother had about ten
minutes of useful strength left (1-10-1).

**Point of view:** Eli narrates, first person, past tense. The one general "you" line (line 6)
describes the mistake; the rescue steps are Eli's own actions plus short imperatives he calls out.

**Safety line kept throughout:** Eli never goes into the water, never stands near the hole, never
pulls his brother by hand. He calls 911, gets flat, stays short of the hole, passes the picks
across the ice, and talks his brother through self-rescue. The brother gets out. No gore.

**Clock:** `DEC · 16:29` (walk) → `16:31` crack, in-water timer `00:00` → 1-10-1 phase strip
→ `OUT` at about minute six → `16:38` blue hour → PART 2 card.

## Narration (paste into ElevenLabs as ONE block)

```
If your brother falls through the ice, don't run to him.

December sunset, a Minnesota lake. My brother cut past the creek mouth to his red shack.

One crack, and he was in. I was forty meters back, with my phone and my ice picks.

Every part of me wanted to sprint out and grab him.

But a sprint puts all your weight on two boot prints, on ice that just broke.

One more crack, and there are two of you in the water.

So I stopped. Cold water runs on one, ten, one.

About one minute of gasping. About ten minutes of useful strength. Up to one hour before passing out.

Enough time to do it right. Not enough to do it twice.

Step one: call. Nine-one-one, three things: the lake's name, the red shack, person in the water.

Step two: reach or throw. I got down flat on my stomach to spread my weight out.

I inched along his footprints, stopped short of the hole, and pushed my ice picks across to him on their cord.

Step three: don't go in after him. My job was to talk him out.

Arms on the ice. Kick your legs up flat. Dig in the picks and pull.

He slid out on his stomach. Don't stand, I said. Roll the way you came.

We rolled away along his footprints as blue hour fell. Soaked, shaking, but out.

Out of the water isn't out of the cold. Part two: the hour after.
```
